# Bearing Export Hero Visual Research

## Question

What visual sequence is credible for large industrial bearing exports: packing in China, loading at the port, and ocean transport?

## Findings

- Bearings are protected against moisture and corrosion before export. Common packaging includes anti-corrosion oil, VCI film or paper, desiccant, shock indicators and a closed wooden export case with internal blocking and steel strapping.
- Bearings are not left exposed on a quay. The packaging/case is the cargo unit and must provide weather protection, load support and lifting points.
- Standard dry containers are the normal unit when packaged bearings fit within internal dimensions and weight limits. A wooden case is internal protection, not the visible unit lifted at the terminal.
- Heavy or over-dimensional bearing assemblies can use flat-rack, open-top or breakbulk arrangements, but require a dedicated lift plan and certified lifting points rather than lifting the wooden package by unauthorised slings.
- Port loading is performed by ship-to-shore gantry cranes with spreaders, slings or lifting frames. Standard containers are lifted at their corner castings; non-standard loads are lashed to a flat rack or lifting frame before sailing.
- A container vessel does not visually expose the bearing after loading: containers are stacked and secured on deck or below deck. The final export image should therefore show a ship underway, not a bearing on the quay or on deck.

## Visual Sequence

1. Factory packing: opened export case, protected bearing, VCI film, desiccant, blocking and strapping. No face visible.
2. Port loading: protective wooden cases packed inside a fully closed steel container, with the container lifted at its corner castings toward a container ship. No exposed bearing or wooden lifting package.
3. Global voyage: container vessel underway from a Northeast Chinese industrial port.

Live search providers and several carrier/manufacturer pages were blocked by bot verification during this session. The scene uses standard heavy-cargo and moisture-protection practice rather than copying an unverified stock image.
